:::section kicker="Game rules" headline="Opponent moves, responses, and spells"
The arena is the smallest fighting game, but that doesn't mean it's easy:
you need to be fast and smart. An ogre faces you. It has many types of
attacks, and before each one it makes a subtle movement (a *telegraph*): it
raises the club, it charges, it staggers with its guard open. As a fighter,
you can respond to its move with five different movements: block high, block
low, dodge, strike, wait. This is not the kind of game that waits for your
turn. You have two seconds to respond before the ogre strikes. If the timer
runs out, you did nothing, and you will very much regret it.

:::figure id="moves-and-responses" caption="Five of the ogre's moves and the response that beats each one, and the spell that does real damage. Read the telegraph, answer within two seconds, and cast when you can."
:::

In the top-left corner of the ring there is a **spell card**: a colored card with
three shapes. Only a spell that matches it does real damage. In the game you can
cast a spell with the buttons under the fight: pick the card's color, then
its shapes from left to right, then press **CAST**. The clock keeps running
while you pick, so you have to build the spell and react to the ogre's
attacks at the same time. The keys 1 to 5 still answer each move. A wrong
spell fizzles. In step 6, Gemini reads the spell card for you.

:::key
A fight is a stream of small decisions with a deadline on each one. That is
what most software automation actually looks like, minus the club.
:::
:::
